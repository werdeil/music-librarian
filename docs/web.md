# Dashboard

`watch --listen [HOST:]PORT` serves a dashboard on that address while it watches the Inbox: the passes running and the recent ones with their output, the schedule, the Inbox and the review questions awaiting an answer. The compose file serves it on port 8080 (`WEB_PORT` in `.env` to change the host's port); without a host, every interface is listened on. `MUSIC_LIBRARIAN_LISTEN` sets the address when `--listen` is not given.

The pages read the run history in the state folder and the Inbox root on each request. The passes the dashboard asks for are queued and run by `watch` itself, between its other jobs, so they never compete with them for the lock. When `watch` runs without `--apply`, a badge at the top says the dashboard is read-only. The footer gives the version, and folds the library, Inbox and state folders under **folders**. While a pass runs or waits in the queue, a pill at the top of every page says so, refreshed every 5 s, and opens on a click onto the passes running, linked to their live output, and those queued.

A pass `watch` runs, from the dashboard or its schedule, can be stopped from its page or the pill: it ends the file it is on, as on `docker stop`, a tool of `enrich` included, then `watch` goes on with the rest of its queue; what the pass did is journaled, so it can be undone. A stopped scheduled pass runs again at its next slot. A request still queued can be cancelled from the history, the pill or its waiting page. A pass started by hand in another container cannot be stopped from here. Its spinner stays still when the system asks for reduced motion.

## Pages

- **Overview**: a **To do** section, refreshed every 5 s, linking each count to where it is handled: the questions each review still awaits (as the Reviews page counts them), the imports left in `_failed/`, the tracks with a field holding a value twice or several values. Only those that need something get a tile, the largest count first, with what to do (**Answer**, **See**, **Fix**); with none, the section says there is nothing to handle. Under them, on one line, the tracks without lyrics, a count that never reaches zero, and what is still being worked out or has no validated genre list; then, on another, what is up to date. Then the items waiting in the Inbox (shown only when there are some, or when the Inbox is missing), the scheduled passes of `watch` (how often the Inbox is read, the nightly `organize` and the weekly `enrich`) with each command's last run and next slot, and a link to the runs with problems of the last 7 days nobody has marked checked, when there are any. Last, the passes it can run: import, organize, enrich and scan, each as a dry run first, its card opening a dialog of its options (but import, which has none); split, dedupe and the genres run from the Reviews page. The Reviews tab of the menu shows the total of the questions on every page.
- **Library**: the albums of the metadata cache, one per folder holding tracks, with their cover (`folder.jpg`, else the first embedded one, else a placeholder) and their number of tracks in its corner, under it their title and album artist, or *Compilation* for an album whose tracks all carry the compilation flag; or, under **Tracks**, a table of their tracks (album, number, title, artist), 100 per page. Both are searched by artist, album, title or folder without case or accents, a track kept when its folder, album artist, album, artist and title hold every word, as the words are typed (the results change in place and the address follows), and sorted by artist (the default), album, year (newest first) or by when a track was last added, removed or renamed in the folder, the tracks in their albums' order. Under the search box, **Advanced search** keeps the tracks by their fields, through conditions that must all hold, or at least one: a text field (any field, title, artist, album artist, album, composer, genre, grouping, folder, or any other tag by its name, such as `isrc`) contains, does not contain, is, is not or starts with a value, is filled, is empty or has several values (several tags, or names separated by `;`); a number (year, track or disc number, duration in minutes) equals, is under, over or between values, is filled or empty; the lyrics (`[Instrumental]` counting as lyrics), the embedded cover, the ReplayGain, a repeated value and the compilation flag are there or not; the format (`m4a`, `mp3`, `flac`…) is or is not one. Text is compared without case or accents, and a condition missing its value is left out. An album shows when one of its tracks is kept, its corner then counting them (`3/12`); the track table adds a column for each field the conditions test. The filters above the results are searches of their own, a click putting their condition in the form, each with the albums, or the tracks, it keeps, and left out when it keeps none unless it is the one shown: the tracks without lyrics, with repeated values, the compilations, those without composer, without genre, without embedded cover and without ReplayGain. The address holds the whole search (`?c=composer:empty&c=year:between:1990:1999&match=any&view=tracks`), to bookmark it; the former `?only=lyrics` still opens its filter, and without script the form's rows are read as posted. **one by one** opens the first album found, to step through them; for the tracks without lyrics or with repeated values, it opens their list. A library never scanned shows nothing until a `scan`; a file moved or deleted since it was cached is left out. The list is built in the background whenever the cache changes, so a page never waits on it: right after `watch` starts, it says it is still being worked out, under a bar of how far the build has read the cache, until the first build ends, and after a change it shows the previous list until the new one is ready; either way the page reloads by itself once the lists it shows are built, without waiting on the others (it asks `/built` every 2 s, and moves the bar), unless something was typed, ticked or unfolded on it meanwhile. The reviews and the lyrics page do the same. The lists are built one at a time, the album list first. While a pass writes the cache, the list is built again at most once a minute, and at once when the pass ends.
- **An album**: its fields shared by every track (album artist, album, year, genre, number of discs, compilation flag) and a grid of its tracks (disc, number, title, artist, composer, grouping, the tracks of each disc under a row holding the disc's name, written to every track of the disc; the discs and the grouping folded behind **Discs and grouping** unless the album is numbered beyond one disc or holds a disc name or a grouping), read from the files themselves, with each track's lyrics (present, instrumental, or neither), its ReplayGain track gain and its format, the file's name showing on hover. See below for editing them. Each track's lyrics open on a page of their own, to edit or delete them, and the album's cover is replaced from its page, see below. A click on a track's gain strikes it out, a second click brings it back: the album form's **Preview the changes** then removes every `REPLAYGAIN_*` tag of the tracks struck out (the track gain and peak `enrich` writes, and any other a tagger left), with the other edits; the run's page shows each track's gain going, an applied pass is undone like any other, the tags written back as they were, and the next `enrich` measures the gain of those tracks again. **Look up the album's lyrics** queues a dry run of `enrich.lyrics` on its folder with `--look-up`, which looks the lyrics up online where the dry run of `enrich` only counts the tracks, and `--retry`, which looks up the tracks found in vain without waiting for `LYRICS_RETRY_DAYS`; the run's page offers to write what it found.
- **Repeated values**, linked from the library and the overview: the albums whose tracks have a field (title, artist, album artist, album, year, genre, composer) holding a value twice, a credit naming someone twice, or several values that differ, according to the metadata cache, the most such tracks first. The weekly `enrich` keeps once what is only repeated; what differs is fixed on the album's page, where those fields are outlined, their tooltip saying which case it is. The form refuses a title or an album holding a `;`.
- **Tracks without lyrics**, linked from the library and the overview: the albums whose tracks carry no lyrics according to the metadata cache, the most such tracks first, with the last time they were looked up in vain within `LYRICS_RETRY_DAYS`. A track marked instrumental is left out; tracks no provider carries stay in it, so it seldom empties.

An album opened from either list, or from a search of the library, keeps it: its page shows a bar with its position in the list, **← Previous** and **Next →** to the albums around it, and a link back to the list. The bar stays through a preview, an apply, an undo and a track's lyrics page. Once fixed, an album leaves the list as soon as the album list is rebuilt; its page then counts the albums left and steps on from where it stood.
- **Reviews**: the questions split and dedupe cannot settle, the genres the validated list does not know and the tracks without lyrics, see below, and the files their answers are kept in.
- **History**: the passes the dashboard queued, at the top of the first page, then the runs kept in the history (the 200 latest, a running one included), 50 per page, filtered by command, and with a checkbox to the runs that failed, stopped, were interrupted or need checking and nobody has marked checked yet. Each of those has a **Mark checked** button, the filtered page one to mark all its runs at once, and a run's page marks it or unmarks it; a run marked checked keeps its status, with a **Checked** badge, and leaves the problems of the overview and of the filter. A pass started by `watch` says why: at startup, for new items in the Inbox, as the day's import (which purges `_done/`), or on its schedule. The Inbox is read every 30 s, but an import runs, and shows in the history, only for one of those.
- **A run**: its mode, who started it (`watch`, the command line), for a pass started from a form of the overview the boxes that were ticked ("none" when none was), its duration and exit code, its summary, and its whole output, each line coloured by its tag (`ERROR`, `CONFLICT` and `FAILED` in red, `SKIPPED`, `WARNING`, `REFUSED`, `UNKNOWN`, `GUARDED`, `REVIEW`, `MULTIPLE`, `STOP` and `LOCKED` in yellow). While the run goes on, the new lines are appended every 2 seconds, and the page reloads with the summary once it ends. "Plain text" gives the output as a file.

Times are the container's (`TZ` in `.env`).

## Running a pass

The overview has a form per command: `import`, `organize` (`--since` ticked by default), `enrich` (`--rescan`, and the genres, the ReplayGain, the covers, the lyrics, the repeated values, the split and the aliases, ticked by default: unticking one passes `--no-genres`, `--no-replaygain`, `--no-covers`, `--no-lyrics`, `--no-repeats`, `--no-split` or `--no-dedupe`, and `--retry`, unticked and set under the lyrics, offered only while they are ticked, which looks up the lyrics found in vain within `LYRICS_RETRY_DAYS` too), `scan` (`--force`), `split` and `dedupe`. Their options are in the dialog the card opens, each box that makes the pass long (`--rescan`, `--retry`, unticking `--since`, `--force`) showing why while it is in that state; each card says that a preview comes first and nothing changes until it is applied (`scan` changes no file); **Preview** then queues the dry run and opens the run's page, which follows it live once `watch` gets to it; while a pass is running, the request waits in the queue, listed on the overview.

A run's page leads, once the pass ended, with its outcome, its summary and what can be done next, the output folded underneath (open while the pass runs), and always a way back to where the pass was started from: the album, the reviews or the overview. The page of a dry run started from the dashboard offers **Apply this pass**: the same command with the same options, applied. It is a new pass, which plans again from the library as it then is. The page of an applied pass that left a journal offers **Undo this pass**, again as a dry run first. A pass queued from an album's page (its fields, a track's lyrics, its cover, the lookup of its lyrics) is followed on that page instead (see below), and its run's page offers **Back to the album**, where the pass filed it. Nothing is applied from the dashboard unless `watch` runs with `--apply`: without it, the dashboard runs dry runs only.

A request that finds the lock taken (a command run by hand, `docker compose run`) is not kept, and its page says so. The queue lives in `watch`: a restart drops the requests not yet started. The `--review` modes of `split` and `dedupe` ask questions and stay on the command line.

## Editing an album

Change any field on the album's page, then **Preview the changes**: the page comes back with the fields as typed and a box listing what a `tag` pass (see `music-librarian tag`) would do, one edit per field changed, an album field applying to every track: each change with its tracks, the folder the tracks move to, and what it would leave alone and why. The preview is a dry run worked out by the dashboard itself, at once, without waiting for a pass under way; nothing is written but the metadata cache. **Apply** then queues the pass applied, which writes the tags and files the tracks where their new tags put them, all in one journal; **Discard** shows the album as it is.

The album's page follows a pass queued from it, refreshed every 2 s: waiting in the queue (with **Cancel**), running, then its outcome with its summary, **Undo** for an applied pass that left a journal (applied at once, itself undoable), and **see the details** for the run's page. A dry run queued from it (a track's lyrics, the cover, the lookup of its lyrics) offers **Apply** in the same place. When the pass moved the album, the page goes to where it is now. A field left as shown is not rewritten, and an album field whose tracks differ shows "various values", left alone unless something is typed in. The genre field offers the validated genres of `state/genres.txt`, and a genre the list knows takes its spelling.

Each edit carries the values the page showed: a track whose field changed in between, by another pass or another tagger, is refused (`REFUSED`) rather than overwritten.

### Lyrics

A track's lyrics open under it from their cell of the album's page, in a text box that belongs to the album's form, with the date of its last lookup in vain within `LYRICS_RETRY_DAYS` when it has none. They are edited, previewed and applied with the rest of the form: **Preview the changes** lists them by their number of lines (`1 line → 2 lines`), an emptied box removes them, and **Apply** writes them through `tag`, journaled, so **Undo** puts them back. Ticking **Instrumental** puts `[Instrumental]` in the box and locks it, the mark that keeps `enrich` from looking the track up; unticking it brings back the text it replaced. **Look up online** asks the providers (`LYRICS_PROVIDERS`) for that track only, at once, and puts what they have in the box, or ticks **Instrumental**, saying where it came from or why nothing was found; nothing is written until the form is applied, and a lookup that found nothing is recorded as a pass records it. A track whose lyrics change is no longer counted as looked up in vain: lyrics removed are looked up again by the next `enrich`, without waiting for `LYRICS_RETRY_DAYS`. An edit made against lyrics that changed since is refused, as for any field.

### Cover

**Preview the new cover** sends an image (32 MB at most), in any format ffmpeg reads (JPEG, PNG, WebP, GIF, BMP, HEIC…), converted to JPEG at once when it is not one, and queues a dry run of `cover` (see `music-librarian cover`) on the album, which the album's page follows: its summary says into how many tracks it would be embedded, the run's page which `folder.jpg` it replaces, then it offers to apply. Applied, the image becomes the album's `folder.jpg`, the previous one being kept in `covers/` in the state folder, and is embedded into the tracks; **Undo this pass** puts the previous `folder.jpg` back, which the next `enrich` embeds again. The image waits in `uploads/` in the state folder until then, removed after 7 days: an older dry run can no longer be applied.

## Reviews

The Reviews page asks the questions of `split --review` and `dedupe --review` (see [splitting.md](splitting.md) and [dedupe.md](dedupe.md)) one at a time, in the same order and with the same suggestion, highlighted:

- a credit holding several names: **Split**, **Keep whole**, or the names to write separated by `;`;
- a group of likely duplicates, each spelling with an album that carries it: the spelling to write them all as, picked among them or typed, or **Different artists**;
- a genre the validated list does not know, the one most tracks carry first: a name of a validated genre, added to its line of the list (the one iTunes suggests is picked, when `enrich.genres` looked the album up with `GENRE_LOOKUP=itunes`), or a validated genre of its own;

**Later** puts the question at the end of its review, after every question never put off, the one put off longest first, as recorded in `state/review-later.json`, so the next visit starts with the questions not seen yet; the page that follows an answer offers to undo it. Each answer is written at once to `state/split-decisions.txt`, `state/artist-aliases.txt` or the genre list (`state/genres.txt`, or `MUSIC_LIBRARIAN_GENRES`), as in the terminal, so the review stops and resumes at will, and an answer that settles questions further down skips them. The queues are built from the metadata cache in the background, again whenever it changes, like the album list: until the first build ends, the counts and the questions say they are still being worked out, and the page reloads by itself once it ends. While a pass writes the cache, they are built again at most once a minute, and at once when the pass ends. Answers rewrite no tag: **Preview these answers** queues `split`, `dedupe` or `enrich.genres` over the library, whose page then offers to apply it and leads back to the reviews, and the weekly `enrich` applies them too.

Each review links to the file its answers are kept in, shown read only: the split decisions (each credit and what is done with it, the ones awaiting an answer included), the artist spellings (the variants rewritten as another name, and the artists known to differ), and the validated genres with the names that stand for each and the tracks of the library it gathers, so a genre no track carries any more stands out. To change a line, edit the file in the state folder; the next pass applies it.

## Statuses

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Running | no end recorded yet, and its process is alive, or ran in another container (`docker compose run`) |
| OK | exit code 0 |
| To check | exit code 2: something was skipped, refused or failed; the output says what |
| Stopped | exit code 4: stopped before the end (`docker stop`, Ctrl-C), the rest is done by the next pass |
| Failed | any other exit code, such as 5 for a failed Lyrion carry-over |
| Interrupted | the pass crashed (its output ends with the traceback) or its process was killed, with no end recorded |

A pass whose process is gone is shown interrupted as soon as the dashboard sees it, when it ran in the same container; `watch` also marks the unfinished runs of its container as interrupted when it starts. A pass that found the lock taken is not kept in the history at all.

## Languages

The pages are in French or English, after the browser's preferred language, English by default. The output of the passes stays in English.

## Security

The dashboard has **no authentication**: it is meant for a trusted home network. Anyone who can reach its port sees the library's paths, the names in the Inbox and the output of every pass, and can run any command, applied when `watch` runs with `--apply`. Each form carries a token of the page, so another website open in a browser of the network cannot post to it. Nor can it read the pages through DNS rebinding, a name of its own pointed at the dashboard's address: the dashboard answers only to an IP address, a name without a dot (`nas`), a name under `.local`, `.lan`, `.home`, `.home.arpa` or `.internal`, and the names listed in `MUSIC_LIBRARIAN_WEB_HOSTS` (`watch --web-hosts`, comma-separated, e.g. `nas.example.org` or a Tailscale name); any other name gets a 403 that names the setting. Never expose the port to the Internet (no port forwarding, no public reverse proxy); for remote access, join the home network through a VPN such as WireGuard or Tailscale.

## Endpoints

| Route | Returns |
|---|---|
| `/` | the overview |
| `/runs`, `/runs?command=enrich`, `/runs?problems=1`, `/runs?page=2` | the history |
| `/runs/<id>` | a run; `POST /runs/<id>/stop` stops it while `watch` runs it |
| `/runs/<id>/log.txt` | its output as plain text |
| `POST /runs/checked` | marks the finished runs `run=<id>` (repeated) as checked, or no longer with `checked=0`; back to the history its `command`, `problems` and `page` name, or to the run with `from=run` |
| `/runs/<id>/log.json?from=<offset>&kind=<kind>` | the complete lines written since byte `offset`, each with its style (`error`, `warning`, `section` or empty), the offset to ask from next, and the run's status; `kind` is the style of the line before `offset`, which an indented line continues |
| `POST /passes` | queues a dry run (`command`, its options, and `journal` for `undo`), or the pass applied with `apply=1`; redirects to the request, or with `album=<folder>` to that album's page following it |
| `POST /runs/<id>/apply` | queues a dashboard dry run again, applied; `album=<folder>` as for `/passes` |
| `/library?q=<words>&sort=<sort>&c=<condition>&match=any&view=tracks&page=<n>` | the albums, or the tracks with `view=tracks`, searched, sorted by `artist`, `title`, `year` or `recent`, kept by each condition `c` (`field:operator[:value[:upper bound]]`, such as `genre:empty`, `artist:contains:moby`, `tag.isrc:filled`, `year:between:1990:1999`), all of them or with `match=any` one; `only=<filter>` stands for the condition of a filter (`lyrics`, `repeats`, `compilations`, `composer`, `genre`, `cover`, `replaygain`) |
| `/library/album?folder=<folder>&request=<id>&list=<list>&at=<n>&search=<address>` | an album, its folder relative to the library root, following the request `id` when given, in the work list `repeats`, `lyrics` or `search` (the library's search whose query is `address`) at position `n` when given; `POST` shows the preview of its edits, or with `apply=1` queues them applied |
| `/library/cover?folder=<folder>&v=<version>` | the album's cover, or a placeholder without one; the pages give the time of its `folder.jpg` as `v`, so a new cover is never shown from the browser's cache |
| `POST /library/album/cover` | queues a dry run of `cover` with the `image` sent for the album `folder`, converted to JPEG, followed on its page |
| `POST /library/track/lyrics/lookup` | `{"text": …, "message": …}`: what the providers have for the track `file` of the album `folder` (its lyrics, `[Instrumental]` or `null`), and the sentence saying so; writes nothing |
| `/library/lyrics` | the albums with tracks without lyrics |
| `/library/repeats` | the albums with tracks whose fields hold a value twice or several values |
| `/reviews`, `/reviews/split?from=<n>`, `/reviews/dedupe?from=<n>`, `/reviews/genres?from=<n>` | the reviews, from the question at position `n`; `POST` records an answer, `POST …/undo` takes it back |
| `/reviews/split/decisions`, `/reviews/dedupe/aliases`, `/reviews/genres/list` | the decisions file, the aliases file and the validated genre list, read only |
| `/requests/<id>` | waits for a queued request, then redirects to its run; `POST /requests/<id>/cancel` takes it out of the queue, back to the album page `album=<folder>` names |
| `/requests/<id>.json?folder=<folder>` | `{"done": …, "album": …}`: whether the request ended, and once it has, the page of the album `folder` where the pass left it |
| `/health` | `{"status": "ok", "version": …}`, for the compose healthcheck |
| `/activity` | the header's pill of the passes running and queued, as an HTML fragment |
| `/tiles` | the overview's To do tiles, as the overview shows them |
| `/built` | `{"ready": …, "percent": …}`: whether the lists the page of `?page=<endpoint>` shows (without it, the album list and every review queue) are built and none is being built again, and how far their builds are on average (100 when none runs) |

## For contributors

The package is `web/`: `app.py` (the overview, the header's activity and the app put together), `security.py` (the names it answers to, the forms' token and the response headers), `history.py` (the run history, a run's page and output, and the forms that queue, apply, cancel or stop a pass), `albums.py` (the library's pages and their forms), `common.py` (what the pages share: times, strings, a page of a list, a refusal, a queued pass), `passes.py` (the queue `watch` runs, how each command is built from the options of `watch`, and the uploaded covers), `library.py` (the albums and tracks from the cache, what a search finds, an album's fields, the edits of its form), `query.py` (the conditions of a search, read from its address, and the tracks they keep), `reviews.py` (the split, dedupe and genre reviews and their routes), `server.py` (waitress in a thread of `watch`), `i18n.py`, `templates/` and `static/`. Flask is only imported with `--listen`.

- Every string shown lives in `web/i18n.py`, in `fr` and `en` with the same keys; a test checks they match.
- Under 640 px of width (a phone), the pages keep to the screen's width: the navigation takes a line of its own, the album's tracks and the scheduled passes show as cards, each track's folded to its number and title behind a button that unfolds the rest (one holding a repeated value starts unfolded), a track's lyrics staying folded until asked for as on a wider screen, and the history leaves out the origin and duration columns.
- The Content Security Policy is `default-src 'self'`: no inline script or style, everything from `static/`.
- The page scripts are plain JavaScript with no framework or build step, linted by `npx --yes eslint@9 src/music_librarian/web/static/*.js` (the CI lint job).
